Cut Planes

Overview

The Cut Plane functionality allows users to slice through the simulation grid to inspect internal cell values while maintaining the full 3D spatial context. Using Cut Planes, you can expose internal layers or view cross‑sections of the grid to better understand spatial variations in properties such as pressure, saturation, permeability, etc.

An example of Cut Plane showing the cell values that intersect with an arbitrary plane.

Accessing the Grid Cut Planes

You can access the Cut Plane Settings through the grid display interface:

  1. Open the Grid Display you wish to customize
  2. Double left-click on the Grid
  3. The Grid Editors dialog will be displayed
  4. Select Cut Planes from the Treeview.

From these settings a Cut Planes can be created and controlled.

What a Cut Plane Does

A Cut Plane is a plane passed through the body of the grid. It can be a single plane, a pair of parallel planes, or a regular stacked or mesh family of planes running through the grid at set intervals.

Wherever a plane intersects the grid, the cell values at that cross-section are displayed. You can choose to show only the cells the plane touches, for a thin slice through the data, or also keep the cells on one side, in-between, or outside the plane(s), so you can peel back part of the grid to reveal what's inside.

Why Use Cut Planes

  • See how a property such as pressure, saturation, or permeability varies inside the reservoir body, not just on its outer surface.
  • Inspect a specific layer or cross-section, for example through a well or across a fault.
  • Isolate a zone of interest by keeping only the slab between a pair of planes, or open up the middle of the grid to reveal the internal structure.
  • Sweep systematically through the whole grid with stacked or mesh planes to QC geometry, layering, or property continuity.
  • Angle a plane freely to align it with a fault or well trajectory, rather than being limited to a fixed axis.

How to Use Cut Plane

Choose a Cut Plane Mode based on what you want to see: a single angled slice, an isolated slab, or a systematic sweep through the grid.

Free-Orientated Planes

  • Single Plane: Set Orientation (XY / XZ / YZ / Arbitrary), then Offset (and Rotation/Tilt if Arbitrary) to place the plane; choose Plane Only / Minus Side / Plus Side to decide how much of the grid stays visible.
  • Plane Pair: Same orientation controls, plus Spacing between the two planes; choose Planes Only / Between / Outside to isolate or remove the slab between them.

Axis-Orientated Planes

  • Stacked X / Y / Z: A series of parallel planes on one axis; set either a plane count (always odd — one at each extremity, one at centre) or a fixed spacing distance.
  • Mesh in X and Y / Mesh in X, Y and Z: Combines two or three stacked axes into a waffle/lattice pattern, each axis configured independently the same way.

Appearance (Show Plane/Normal, colour, Transparency) applies regardless of mode and can be adjusted at any time.
