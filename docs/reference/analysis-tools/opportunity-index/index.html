Opportunity Index

Overview

The Opportunity Index combines three normalised grid properties into a single "sweet spot" indicator that highlights zones of remaining reservoir potential — good flow capacity, good remaining moveable volume, and retained pressure support, all at once. It is calculated separately for oil (OPPO) and gas (OPPG), for Eclipse-format grid data.

Use it as a first-pass screening tool during mature-field surveillance, to flag candidate zones for infill drilling, recompletion, or restimulation or to track how a sweet spot shrinks or migrates over time, before moving to detailed, well-specific volumetric or economic evaluation of any individual cell.

Accessing the Opportunity Index

The Opportunity Index is calculated automatically on load when the loaded Eclipse case contains the required properties, and appears as an Opportunity Index node in the Project View, grouped by grid and timestep. Settings are opened from the Show Opportunity Index Settings button on the Opportunity Index node of the Project View.

Required Data

If the node does not appear for a case, check the following properties were exported from Eclipse and loaded:

  • Static/INIT properties: PERMX, PERMY, PERMH (horizontal permeability), NTG, DZ
  • Restart properties, per timestep: SOIL/SGAS, PRESSURE, plus the critical/residual saturations SOWCR (oil) and SGCR (gas)
  • Relative permeability curves for OILKR/GASKR

The Three Components

The Opportunity Index calculation produces three sets of map properties:

  • Relative Flow Capacity: Identifies zones with the greatest capacity to flow fluid.

Calculation: Horizontal Permeability × Relative Permeability × Net-to-Gross × Net Thickness,

(PERMH/PERMX/PERMY × OILKR/GASKR × NTG × DZ)

  • Mobile Fluid Volume: Identifies zones still holding moveable hydrocarbon.

Calculation: Pore Volume × Mobile Saturation, i.e. saturation above the critical/residual saturation,

(SOIL−SOWCR for oil, SGAS−SOGCR for gas)

  • Pressure Support: Identifies zones that have retained reservoir energy rather than depleting.

Calculation: Ratio of Current to Initial Pressure.

Combining and Normalising

Each component is normalised to a 0–1 scale before combining, using automatically-calculated lower/upper thresholds (the lower pick sits 75% of the way from the average down to the minimum across all timesteps; the upper pick sits 33% of the way from the average up to the maximum). The three normalised components are then combined via a geometric mean, i.e.:

Opportunity Index = (FlowNormalised x VolumeNormalised x PressureNormalised)1/3

The index is only high where all three factors are favourable; a cell strong in two components but weak in the third will still score low overall. For example, a cell with excellent permeability-thickness but pressure depleted to 20% of its initial value will still pull down to a low combined score, since the weak Pressure Support term drags the geometric mean down regardless of how strong the other two components are.

Note: because the thresholds are calculated once across all timesteps rather than per-timestep, the colour scale stays fixed through time. This is what makes the index useful for surveillance, the same value at two different timesteps means the same thing, so a sweet spot can be watched shrinking, growing or migrating across the field's production history rather than being re-scaled and re-coloured at every step.

The Opportunity Index Settings window lists the lower/upper threshold pair per component, split by phase (Oil/Gas). Values are in the same units as the underlying Eclipse property (e.g. mD·ft-equivalent for Flow Capacity, reservoir volume units for Mobile Volume, psia for Pressure).

Settings

The Opportunity Index Settings window lists the lower/upper threshold for each component, separately for Oil and Gas. Each threshold can be typed in directly or reset. The thresholds can be reset to their auto-calculated values with the Reset All button. Adjusting the thresholds changes the contrast of the index. Narrowing them exaggerates differences between cells, widening them smooths the result.

Close will close the window.

Interpreting the Index

A value near 1 marks a "sweet spot," a strong flow capacity, meaningful remaining volume, and retained pressure. This makes it a useful starting point for identifying bypassed pay, infill drilling targets, or candidates for further stimulation. A value near 0 means at least one of the three components is poor in that cell.

Limitations: The Opportunity Index is a relative screening indicator, not a volumetric or economic ranking. It says nothing about well spacing, drainage from existing wells, completion feasibility, faulting or compartmentalisation, or the economics of accessing a given cell. Treat a high value as a prompt to investigate further with full reservoir engineering analysis, not as a stand-alone recommendation to drill or recomplete.

Like any other grid property, the Opportunity Indices can be displayed in a Grid Display and used for further analysis or Region definitions.
